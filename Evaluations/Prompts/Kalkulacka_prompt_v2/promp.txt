## Objective

Create a calculator that evaluates mathematical expressions containing the four basic arithmetic operators and parentheses. The implementation must correctly follow standard mathematical operator precedence and return the numerical result as a floating-point value.

## Functional Requirements

1. **Addition (`+`)** — Add two numbers.
2. **Subtraction (`-`)** — Subtract the right operand from the left operand.
3. **Multiplication (`*`)** — Multiply two numbers.
4. **Division (`/`)** — Divide the left operand by the right operand.
5. **Parentheses (`(` and `)`)** — Evaluate expressions inside parentheses before applying operations outside them. Support nested parentheses.
6. **Operator precedence** — Multiplication and division must have higher precedence than addition and subtraction.
7. **Left-to-right evaluation** — Operators with equal precedence must be evaluated from left to right.
8. **Decimal results** — Support fractional results without rounding or truncating them unnecessarily.
9. **Numeric literals** — Support integer and decimal numbers, including expressions such as `1/8`, `5/2`, and `7/4`.
10. **Whitespace** — Ignore leading, trailing, and internal whitespace between tokens.
11. **Input validation** — Handle malformed expressions and division by zero explicitly, using appropriate exceptions supported by the target language. Do not silently return an incorrect result.

## Expression Grammar

The calculator must support the following grammar:

- `expression` = `term` followed by zero or more `+ term` or `- term` operations.
- `term` = `factor` followed by zero or more `* factor` or `/ factor` operations.
- `factor` = a numeric literal or a parenthesized expression.

This grammar ensures that multiplication and division take precedence over addition and subtraction, while operations of equal precedence are evaluated from left to right.

If the target language and requirements support it, also handle unary plus and unary minus, including expressions such as `-5`, `+3`, `-(2+3)`, and `2*-3`, without breaking the required operator precedence.
